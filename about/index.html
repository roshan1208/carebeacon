How CareBeacon works

Last data update 16 September 2026. Page generated 16 September 2026.

What this site is

The regulators of care homes in the UK publish today's picture: the current rating or grade, the current registered manager, whether a home is registered. They do not publish what changed. CareBeacon keeps the picture from each update and shows the differences — a home downgraded, a home that lost its registration, a new manager, a new inspection — so that a family, a council commissioner or an adviser can see movement, not just a snapshot. Everything here is derived from the regulators' own published data.

Sources, by country

Every country covered, and every country not yet covered with the reason.

  • England15,471 registered care homes from the Care Quality Commission's Syndication API, checked daily. CQC rates on a four-point scale — Outstanding, Good, Requires improvement, Inadequate — overall and for five key questions. Changes shown: rating up or down, first rating, registration lost, registration regained. The public change window on this site is 90 days; 1,738 operator pages cover groups running two or more homes.
  • Scotland1,380 registered care homes from the Care Inspectorate's monthly datastore (latest snapshot 31 August 2026). The Care Inspectorate grades 1 (Unsatisfactory) to 6 (Excellent) on up to five quality themes; the grade shown for a home is its lowest current theme grade, which is how the Care Inspectorate itself summarises a service. Changes shown: grade up or down, first grading, new registration, cancellation. Because the Care Inspectorate keeps an archive, each Scottish home's page carries its grade history back to 31 March 2015 — annual to 2025, monthly since — and a change in an annual window is shown as such, never as a day the source did not give.
  • Northern Ireland468 registered nursing and residential care homes from RQIA's register on Open Data NI (latest file 3 August 2026, updated roughly monthly). RQIA inspects and reports but does not rate or grade, so no quality badge is shown. Changes shown: new registration, cancellation, a change of registered manager, a new inspection. There is no archive, so history begins with the first file this site recorded.
  • Wales1,481 registered care home services from Care Inspectorate Wales's directory export (latest 16 September 2026; CIW refreshes it daily). CIW rates four themes — well-being, care and support, environment, leadership and management — as Excellent, Good, Requires Improvement or Requires Significant Improvement, and publishes no overall rating; where one grade appears here it is the lowest of the four themes, labelled as our summary. Changes shown: a theme rating up or down, first rating, new registration, a service leaving the register, a change of responsible individual. Ratings began in April 2025, so many services are not yet rated. There is no archive; history begins with the first export this site recorded.
  • Ireland547 designated centres for older people from HIQA's register export (latest 16 September 2026; checked daily). HIQA inspects and publishes reports but does not rate or grade, so no quality badge is shown. Changes shown: new registration, cancellation, a change in the conditions of registration, a change of person in charge, a change in registered places, a renewed registration, a Chief Inspector's note (so far, notice of intention to close). There is no archive; history begins with the first register this site recorded.
  • United States14,690 nursing homes from CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) (latest 14 September 2026; CMS refreshes Care Compare monthly). CMS rates each nursing home 1 to 5 stars overall and on three components. Changes shown: the overall stars up or down, a component rating changing, special focus status, the abuse icon, an ownership change, fines, a home certified or terminated. Monthly archives exist back to 2019; history here begins with the first release this site recorded. CMS data is a U.S. Government work in the public domain.
  • France7,417 EHPAD (care homes for dependent older people) from FINESS (ANS, Ministry of Health) (latest 16 September 2026; checked daily; FINESS is exported daily). France publishes no rating as open data. Changes shown: the managing legal entity, a name change, an EHPAD opening or leaving the active register. Each establishment links to its FINESS record. FINESS data re-used under the Licence Ouverte / Open Licence 2.0.
  • Japan13,556 residential long-term care facilities from Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) (latest 14 September 2026; the MHLW publishes twice a year, as at 30 June and 31 December). The Ministry publishes no rating. Changes shown: capacity, the operating corporation, a name change, a facility appearing on or leaving the register. Names are shown in Japanese as the Ministry publishes them; each facility links to its own page on the Ministry's disclosure system. MHLW open data re-used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.
  • South Korea6,263 residential long-term care facilities from National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) (latest 14 September 2026; NHIS publishes evaluation results once a year). NHIS grades each evaluated facility A to E with a score out of 100. Changes shown: the grade up or down, a new evaluation, a facility appearing in or leaving the published results. Names are shown in Korean as NHIS publishes them. Public data portal (data.go.kr) dataset with no usage restrictions.
  • Hong Kong1,103 residential care homes for the elderly from Social Welfare Department (latest 16 September 2026; the SWD updates the list daily). The SWD does not grade homes. Two flags from its list are shown: accreditation under the Residential Care Homes accreditation scheme, and a conviction record under the Residential Care Homes (Elderly Persons) Ordinance. Changes shown: either flag changing, place types changing, a home appearing on or leaving the list. Names and addresses are shown in English and Traditional Chinese as the SWD publishes them. Social Welfare Department data re-used under the terms of use of data.gov.hk.
  • New Zealand685 aged residential care premises from HealthCERT (Ministry of Health) (latest 15 September 2026; checked daily; the Ministry updates the file as certificates change). HealthCERT certifies each provider for 12, 24, 36 or 48 months after an audit; a longer period reflects a better outcome, and that period is the only summary measure the Ministry publishes. Changes shown: the period up or down, a new certificate, bed numbers, the legal entity, the auditor, new premises, premises leaving the list. Ministry of Health data re-used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.
  • Belgium (Flanders)812 woonzorgcentra (residential care homes) from Departement Zorg (Flemish Department of Care) (latest 16 September 2026; checked daily; the Department refreshes the export as records change). The Department does not grade homes. Changes shown: recognised places, the average daily price a resident pays, the governing body (inrichtende macht), a home recognised or leaving the register. Provinces are derived from postcodes. Flemish Department of Care open export; reuse terms as published on zorg-en-gezondheid.be.

Update cadence

England is refreshed every day from CQC's change feed, Wales every day from CIW's directory export, and Ireland every day from HIQA's register export. Scotland and Northern Ireland are refreshed the day a new register file appears, which is roughly monthly. Every page states the date of the data it shows.

What we do not do

  • We do not inspect, rate or recommend care homes. Ratings and grades are the regulators' own published judgements, reproduced as published.
  • We do not collect reviews, prices or availability.
  • We do not hold personal data about residents. Registered managers' names appear only where the regulator publishes them.
  • A change on this site is the difference between two of a regulator's published states. If the regulator corrects its data, the change here may be a correction, not an event.
  • Distances on English home pages are straight-line, not by road.
  • Map positions and distances use the regulator's own coordinates where it publishes them (England, Japan, France, the United States). Elsewhere a home is placed from its postcode via postcodes.io or from its address via OpenStreetMap Nominatim (© OpenStreetMap contributors). Where only a town, district or county could be found, the position is approximate and the page says so.

Licences and independence

All source data is reused under the Open Government Licence v3.0. CareBeacon is independent of, and not endorsed by, the Care Quality Commission, the Care Inspectorate, CIW, RQIA, HIQA, CMS, the ANS, the MHLW, the NHIS, Hong Kong's SWD, New Zealand's Ministry of Health, the Flemish Department of Care, or any care provider. Irish data is re-used under Ireland's Public Sector Information regulations, as HIQA's site permits. If something here looks wrong, the regulator's own record — linked from every home's page — is the authority.
