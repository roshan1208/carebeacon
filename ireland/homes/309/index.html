HIQA (the Health Information and Quality Authority) registers and inspects Ireland's nursing homes as designated centres for older people. It does not rate or grade them, so no quality badge is shown here. Its inspection reports are published per centre on hiqa.ie. What this page shows is the register itself, and what changed in it.

Conditions of registration (5)

2 more than the three standard conditions almost every centre carries — HIQA attaches extra conditions where it has restricted something about the centre.

  1. Subject to any prohibitions or restrictions contained in any other condition(s), the designated centre shall be operated at all times in accordance with the Statement of Purpose received 5 May 2026 within the footprint of the designated centre on the floor plan received on 15 September 2025, excluding the use of 21 beds on the first floor of the designated centre. Although the centre is comprised of 63 bedrooms, consisting 52 single bedrooms and 11 twin-bedrooms, this registration decision allows the use of 43 beds on the ground floor and 10 beds on the first floor. The registered provider shall only provide for the specific care and support needs, and services, within the facilities as set out the Statement of Purpose, as agreed with the Chief Inspector at the time of registration. Any changes to the specific care and support needs and services provided must be agreed in advance with the Chief Inspector.
  2. Only persons aged 18 years or older shall be accommodated at the designated centre at any time.
  3. The maximum number of persons that may be accommodated at the designated centre is: 53.
  4. The registered provider shall, by 30 June 2026, have in place a governance and management structure and personnel with the knowledge, competence and skills required to supervise the delivery of care to residents as evidenced by:a. Effective supervision of all aspects of resident care and operation of the designated centre, including appropriate governance, supervision, and support structures to ensure that all staff are adequately supervised and supported in the performance of their duties.b. The appropriate organisation, management and supervision of the staffing resources to ensure an appropriate and consistent number and skill-mix of staff, having regard to the assessed needs of the residents and the size and layout of the centre, to ensure the effective delivery of all aspects of residents’ care and social care needs.c. Appropriate care plans for all residents, underpinned by comprehensive assessments of the residents care needs, and in response to residents changing care needsd. The provision of appropriate and timely access to medical and health care for residents, including a high standard of evidenced-based nursing care in accordance with professional guidelines.e. Improved compliance with key regulations of the Health Act 2007 (Care and Welfare of Residents in Designated Centres for Older People) Regulations 2013, which underpin resident care specifically:I. Regulation 05: Individual Assessment and Care PlanningII. Regulation 06: Health CareIII. Regulation 08: ProtectionIV. Regulation 09: Residents' RightsV. Regulation 15: StaffingVI. Regulation 16: Training and Staff DevelopmentVII. Regulation 21: RecordsVIII. Regulation 23: Governance & Management
  5. No new residents may be admitted or transferred into the designated centre until the registered provider has demonstrated to the satisfaction of the Chief Inspector that;i. An appropriate governance and management structure is established and maintained within the designated centre, including the appointment of a full-time person in charge who has the qualifications, experience, knowledge, competence and skills required to ensure effective governance, operational management, and oversight of the quality and safety of the care and support provided to residents. ii. There is a defined management structure with clearly defined lines of authority, accountability and responsibility within the governance and management structure to ensure effective oversight of all aspects of the service, including the delivery of safe, effective and person-centred care, regulatory compliance, and quality assurance within the designated centre.
